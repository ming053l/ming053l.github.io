TL;DR: ReplayMark adds a hidden watermark by favoring model responses that match a secret key. To verify text later, replay the original model and measure that agreement.

The idea

Where the green and red labels come from

Green means the group favored by the secret key. Red is the other group. These labels describe the watermark preference.

In a word-list method, a hash uses the context and key to assign candidate words to the two lists. In ReplayMark, the model predicts with two different parts of the earlier context hidden. The key chooses which direction of change counts as green.

Technical detail: compare the generation procedures

For a closer look, compare the ordinary decoder with the extra steps ReplayMark adds.

Generic dLLM Decoding

Require: prompt x, model θ, decoder F

1  y ← masked continuation
2  while masked positions remain:
3    p ← model predictions for y
4    J ← positions chosen by F
5    for i in J:
6      v ← reference token draw from pᵢ
7      yᵢ ← v
8  return y

ReplayMark: Probe and Write

Require: same x, θ, F; key K, R, κ

1  y ← masked continuation
2  while masked positions remain:
3    p ← model predictions for y
4    J ← same reference schedule F
5    for i in J:
6      rebuild probes; select carrier
7      if carrier: consult keyed direction
8        try ≤ R plausible matching draws
9        otherwise: unchecked fallback draw
10     else: reference token draw
11     yᵢ ← selected token
12 return y

Conceptual pseudocode. Carriers are selected independently of the keyed direction; accepted candidates must have probability at least κ times the live maximum. Verification re-masks the finished text, rebuilds the probes and carriers, and applies the exact binomial-tail test. See the paper for the complete construction.

How it works

How ReplayMark writes and verifies a watermark

The provider uses the key while writing. Later, the verifier repeats the prediction experiment with the original model.

  1. 1

    Find usable responses

    Hide different parts of the earlier context and compare the predictions. Keep positions where plausible words support both directions of change.

  2. 2

    Favor the keyed direction

    After selecting a position, let the key choose the green direction. Favor a plausible word with that response. The model keeps its ordinary order for filling missing words.

  3. 3

    Replay and count

    Use the finished text to reconstruct the hidden-context predictions. Count the responses that agree with the key and measure how surprising that agreement would be by chance.

Why the statistical test works

For text produced without the document key, each selected response has a 50–50 chance of matching. The exact binomial test measures the probability of getting at least the observed number of matches by chance.

p = Pr[Binomial(n, ½) ≥ T]

The guarantee assumes independent hash domains and text produced without the document key. Carrier selection must not use the keyed direction.

Scope & limitations

Requirements and limits

Verification needs the original model and the registered key. Providers or auditors with that access can reconstruct the response groups.

Original model access

Use the same checkpoint, numerical precision, key, and probe settings. The reported 512-token setup needs 144 model calls to verify—about 27 seconds on a TITAN RTX.

Rewriting can erase the signal

In the reported editing study, detection holds up better when edits stay together. Scattered edits hurt more, and full paraphrasing removes detection. Short fragments also provide less evidence.

A positive result is evidence of agreement with the registered watermark. Requester identity and passage-level attribution are outside its scope. The study covers two models and small test cohorts.
