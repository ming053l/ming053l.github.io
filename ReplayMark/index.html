The idea

Can a model recognize the mark
in its own response?

A watermark helps establish where generated text came from. But diffusion language models fill in many positions in parallel, following their own confidence-based order. A scheme designed for left-to-right generation does not naturally fit this process.

ReplayMark starts from a different observation: hide part of the context, and the same model consistently changes its token probabilities. Those changes provide two response directions. A secret key chooses which direction to favor while the model generates; later, a verifier recreates the same conditions and checks for agreement.

How it works

Probe. Write. Replay.

A carrier is a text position where plausible tokens support both response directions. Choose the carriers first; consult the keyed direction second.

  1. 1

    Probe the context

    Re-mask the current block and suffix, then hide different parts of the completed prefix. Compare the model’s token probabilities and retain positions with two viable response directions.

  2. 2

    Write a keyed response

    At a carrier, draw up to R candidates from the live model distribution. Accept the first plausible token in the keyed direction; otherwise, use an unchecked draw. The model’s commit order remains unchanged.

  3. 3

    Replay and count

    Re-mask the finished text and query the same checkpoint. Reconstruct the carriers, count responses that agree with the key, and calculate an exact binomial-tail p-value.

From responses to evidence

How many matches are more than chance?

For text written without the document key, each carrier agrees with its keyed direction with probability ½. When carrier selection is independent of that direction, the total match count follows an exact binomial distribution.

Scope & limitations

Evidence for a registered checkpoint.

Designed for a provider or auditor who can replay the model—not for screening arbitrary text without model access.

What verification needs

The same checkpoint, numerical precision, document key, and probe construction. At 512 tokens, verification takes 144 forward passes—about 27 seconds on a TITAN RTX in the reported setup.

Where edits matter

In the editing study, contiguous re-denoising of 10% or 30% of tokens retains 0.88 TPR. Scattering 10% of edits lowers it to 0.50, and full paraphrasing removes detection. Short fragments also offer fewer carriers.

A low p-value is evidence of agreement with the registered key and replay procedure. It does not identify the requester or attribute individual passages. The study covers two masked DLMs and small document cohorts; broader validation remains future work.
